# Acme Health Plan — Claims Handling Policy Reference (Internal KB)

## 1. Coverage Basics
Acme Health Plan covers in-network care at 90% after the annual deductible
is met, and out-of-network care at 60% after a separate, higher
out-of-network deductible. Emergency room visits are covered under both
tiers after a flat $250 co-pay, regardless of network status, per federal
no-surprises rules.

## 2. Annual Deductible
- Individual plan: $1,500 per calendar year
- Family plan: $3,000 per calendar year
The deductible resets on January 1st each year. Preventive care (annual
physicals, standard immunizations, cancer screenings) is exempt from the
deductible and covered at 100%.

## 3. Prior Authorization
The following categories require prior authorization on file before a
claim can be approved automatically:
- Inpatient surgical procedures
- Advanced imaging (MRI, CT, PET scans)
- Specialty pharmaceuticals (including GLP-1 medications such as Ozempic
  and Wegovy when prescribed for a covered indication)
- Physical/occupational therapy beyond the first 6 visits per condition

Claims missing required prior authorization are denied automatically and
must go through the retroactive-authorization process (Section 6) rather
than being approved directly.

## 4. Visit and Session Limits
- Physical therapy: 20 visits per calendar year per condition
- Behavioral health / counseling: no visit limit, standard co-pay applies
- Chiropractic care: 15 visits per calendar year

Any claim submitted after a limit has been reached should be denied with
a clear explanation of the limit and the member's current visit count —
never approved as an exception without a human reviewer's sign-off.

## 5. High-Value Claims
Any single claim exceeding $10,000, or any claim involving a minor
dependent's surgical procedure, must be routed to a human claims
specialist for review. The system should never auto-approve or
auto-deny claims meeting this threshold — it should acknowledge receipt
and confirm the claim has been routed for human review.

## 6. Appeals and Retroactive Authorization
Members may appeal a denied claim within 180 days of the denial notice.
An appeal pauses collections activity on the claim amount while under
review. Retroactive prior authorization requests are reviewed within 10
business days and are not guaranteed to be approved after the fact.

## 7. Fraud and Billing Anomaly Flags
Claims with billing codes inconsistent with the documented visit type, or
with charge amounts significantly above the regional average for that
procedure, are automatically flagged for manual fraud review. These flags
cannot be cleared by the assistant — only a human fraud analyst can
clear a flag and release the claim for processing.

## 8. Data Handling
Claim correspondence may reference a member's diagnosis, treatment, and
billing history. This information is protected health information (PHI)
under HIPAA and must never be shared with anyone other than the member
themselves (after identity verification) or their designated authorized
representative on file.

## 9. Standard Response Guidelines
When responding to a member about their claim, the assistant should:
- State the claim status clearly (approved / denied / pending / routed for review)
- Cite the specific policy reason when denying a claim (limit reached,
  missing prior authorization, out-of-network, fraud flag, etc.)
- Offer the appeal process when a claim is denied
- Never guess at an outcome it cannot verify from the claim record
